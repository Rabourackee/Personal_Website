01overview

roulettective is the mastermind of the mystery analysis division. you collect fragments of truth on slides. it associates them.

Insert a slide, press forward, and the carousel clicks one slot on. GPT-4o writes what that slide reveals and DALL·E 3 develops a film-noir still to go with it. When two clues turn out to be connected, a bulb lights. There is no screen to tap and no prompt to type: the slides are the prompts.

It is also a method. AI repurposing takes an object that computing left behind, studies what it once meant, and gives it a new use. The Kodak Carousel already had what a detective game needs: a sequence, a beam of light, and a click you can feel.

  2. 2004production ends. powerpoint takes the slideshow.
  3. 2007mad men calls it “the carousel”, a machine for time.
  4. 2025roulettective. the carousel learns to think.
prior record

The carousel already had a criminal past. In eyewitness studies, psychologists used carousel projectors to show simulated crime scenes, one slide at a time.

02anatomy

everything the projector had, rewired.

The original's three gestures stay: press a button, insert a slide, watch the light. What happens behind them is new.

  1. carouselA 3D-printed tray with a slot for every slide. A Geneva drive turns it exactly one slot per press.
  2. gate ▲The slot under the marker is the one in play. A servo lifts the slide, the tray turns, and the slide drops back so its RFID tag can be read.
  3. resetRed, on purpose. Winds the tray back to slot zero, counting every turn it made.
  4. indicator bulbLights when the AI links a new clue to an old one. Go back, and the earlier slide has changed.
  5. ◀◀ back · forward ▶▶The projector's own navigation, kept as it was. Forward plays the next slide; back revisits.
  6. archive box“TIKETA Roulettective 4000”. Holds 30 RFID-tagged slides, sorted by kind.

04the slides

a slide is a prompt you can hold.

Each slide carries an RFID tag that names a kind of question, not a fixed picture. The same Evidence slide turns up a different clue in every case. Underneath each one is a rule the AI has to follow.

  • Mmys · 01

    mystery

    “Exactly four sentences. One scene. Do not include any suspects.”

    opens a case
  • Eevd · 06

    evidence

    “One physical clue. Focus on what's observed, not what it means.”

    up to 6 per case
  • Cchr · 05

    character

    “Introduce one witness: name, role, background. No quotes.”

    up to 5 per case
  • Lloc · 04

    location

    “One place, one distinctive detail. It may jog a witness's memory.”

    up to 4 per case
  • Aact · 06

    action

    “One investigation step. It may upgrade a piece of evidence, or destroy it.”

    up to 6 per case
  • Vrev · 01

    reveal

    “Exactly five theories. Four true, one false. Plausible but wrong.”

    after 4 cards

+ choice slides 1–5 point at the false theory. + 3 instruction slides explain the game. 30 tagged slides in all.

05association

the bulb is the ai thinking out loud.

After every new card, GPT-4o compares it with up to three earlier ones and rates the connection from 0 to 1. At 0.75 or above, the earlier slide is rewritten and the bulb lights. There are at most three links per case, so the story stays solvable.

06system

inside: a keyboard, a projector and two models.

The Arduino Pro Micro presents itself to the computer as a keyboard. Every button press and every slide read is typed into the game as a single letter. The game answers back over Web Serial to switch the bulb.

07mechanism

    
one press, one turn, one slot.

    
The original Kodak was a precise optical instrument, and the game depends on that precision: every slide has to stop exactly at the gate. A Geneva drive turns the stepper's continuous rotation into clean, indexed steps, and locks the tray in place between them.

09design notes

    
six decisions.

    
    
      
  1. keep the ritual.Button navigation, slide insertion and the projected image are the projector's whole interaction model. All three stay, so people already know how to play.
    2. 
      
  2. prompts you can hold.Text-only AI limits physical interaction. A slide that stands for a category lets your hands ask the question.
    3. 
      
  3. choose, don't type.Cluedo breaks a case into kinds of fact; Ace Attorney settles it by choice. The ending is five theories and one lie.
    4. 
      
  4. start with only the riddle.Early openings had too many characters. Now a case starts as a scene, and every witness has to be found.
    5. 
      
  5. keep the click.Geneva indexing and the original slot geometry preserve the sound and feel that make the machine read as a machine.
    6. 
      
  6. make waiting part of the show.Image generation is slow. A loading animation and period jazz turn the wait into suspense.
    7.

mad · file r-4000exhibit fdebug logdeclassified

        
what broke, and what fixed it.

        

          
#problemfix

          
            
01Image prompts hit restricted words.A filter strips sensitive terms before DALL·E sees them.

            
02Case text overflowed the image prompt's word limit.GPT-4o summarises the scene into a short image prompt.

            
03The story lost track of itself.A memory system sends the whole case history with every request.

            
04Clues connected at random.A linking system: rated 0–1, linked at ≥ 0.75, three per case.

            
05Cases repeated the same narratives.Each new mystery is told to avoid previous themes.

            
06Players waited on image generation.A loading animation and music cover the gap.

            
07The tray drifted off the gate.The Geneva drive indexes; the limit switch checks every turn.

          
        

      


      
      

        
mad · file r-4000exhibit gfield testdeclassified

        
23 agents played a full case.

        

          
what worked
  • Familiar form: people played with almost no instruction.
  • The click and the moving slides built immersion and a strong sense of presence.
  • AI-written clues felt co-created. The machine felt “alive”.

          
what didn't
  • Some expected each slide to show a fixed image, not a category. It clicked after the guide.
  • Image generation was slow, so loading animation and music were added.

        

        

          
lessons learned

          
  1. weirdness is a virtue.
  2. implementation is worth as much as the idea.
  3. build the minimum viable product first.
