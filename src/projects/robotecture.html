heights in cm. The motor turns its joint to α. 180° is level; the mechanism allows 90° to 270°, so a unit can never be more than one cell (20 cm) above or below a neighbour, and its full range equals its distance to the frame. The beams telescope as the angle changes: watch their lengths.

05

three units, one design

simple, efficient, robust: each round removed something.

  1. v1 · overlapping joints

    a sphere

    Four beams met at the centre of a sphere, guided by a ring gear inside it. Elegant from outside, but the printed PLA sphere deformed within days, and the crowded inside left no room for strong motors.

  2. v2 · independent layers

    beams in layers

    Beams from the two directions stacked in different layers, with no ring gear. It worked, but it was fragile. One version put a camera on every unit, so people could “grab” a unit in the air and pull it up or down.

  3. v3 · the final unit

    stronger, simpler

    A stiffer chassis, larger MG996R motors, lighter and stronger beams, and upgraded power and frames: stable enough to grow into larger grids.

06

like lego, at room scale

every unit is identical. add one by joining its beams to the grid; the frame is built from modular parts too.

control

a laptop computes the shape and the angles; an arduino sends them to eighteen servos.

Applications run in Unity and WebXR. Over serial, an Arduino UNO receives each unit’s height and drives the motors through two 16-channel PCA9685 boards. A 12 V, 50 A supply feeds two 6 V, 25 A converters and the distribution boards.

08

what it becomes

enclosures, surfaces you touch, and objects it handles: the scenarios built with the 1 × 2, 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 prototypes. most can run on the model above.

aarchitecture: enclosures that adapt

  1. opera house

    The ceiling rises into a dome to reflect the singer’s voice to the audience.

  2. a ceiling within reach

    The node holding a broken light bulb comes down to be changed; the same for paintings or anything stored overhead.

  3. solar roof

    The roof follows the sun’s direction, so its panels collect more energy.

  4. rain collection

    When it rains, the roof dips in the middle and gathers the water. Beads stood in for rain.

  5. a wall that moves

    A 1 × 2 standing as a wall between two rooms: it recedes from the occupied room to give it more space.

bsurfaces you touch: furniture and haptics

  1. reading table

    Put a book on it, and the table lifts a slope under it for easier reading.

  2. a bed that wakes you

    When the alarm rings, the bed folds into a chair and sits you up. With pressure sensing, it could fit the body it holds.

  3. terrain, projected

    A mountain road for a toy car: the surface makes the hills, projection mapping paints them.

  4. touch in vr

    A rocky landscape in VR, shaped in the room: the hand finds the rocks where the eyes see them.

cobjects it handles

  1. to your hand

    The surface tilts and the ball slides toward the hand that waits for it.

  2. storage that hides and reveals

    A 7 cm ball rests below the surface. When the beams lower, the slit in the fabric opens and the ball comes up through it; push it back down to hide it.

  3. a maze, from far away

    Over a network, a phone tilts the surface to roll a ball: tangible telepresence.

09

limits, and what’s next

what the prototypes taught, from the paper’s discussion.

  1. height

    The range is bound by the grid, and neighbours can never meet at a right angle. An origami-like unit could fold a floor up into a wall.

  2. weight and sag

    Self-lifting puts the most load on the outer units, and small play in every joint adds up as a sag at the centre. Smaller grids joined by rigid frames keep their strength; standing a grid upright puts the weight on the beams instead.

  3. resolution

    The MG996R sets the size of a unit. Smaller servos would make a denser grid, but a weaker one: Robotecture is meant for tables and rooms, not hand-held displays.

  4. sensing

    It shapes, but does not yet feel. Torque sensors on the motor shafts could let it read a push, or the shape of an object pressed into it.

the upgradeRDS5180 servos with aluminium or carbon-fibre beams: by the paper’s estimate, 1.2 kg at the centre of a 10 × 10 grid, for about $80 a unit.
