序

Prelude

What if a brushstroke were a word, and the word the gesture that brings a picture into being?

Dream Brush is a generative painting experience. You write Chinese characters with a real calligraphy brush, dipped only in water, on a canvas that stands on an easel. The canvas shows the trace of your stroke, then lets it fade as if drying. In its place the system paints: each character becomes a motif of Chinese painting, with sound.

Generative tools are pushing creative work toward detached prompting. Chinese characters offer another way in. A single mark can carry meaning and still be a gesture and a picture, so what you write, where you write it and how large all steer what appears.

写

Try it

Write, then wait.

A working model of the canvas. Choose a character and write it anywhere on the paper, with a mouse, a finger or a pen, or tap and it is written for you. Where you write, and how large, is where and how large the painting changes.

Choose a character, then write it on the paper.

A model, not the installation. These motifs are painted by your browser, and nothing here reads your handwriting. The installation recognises the written character with a vision model and paints it with an image model.

法

Four rules

One character, four decisions.

Each character is read through four coupled constraints. One gesture carries what a screen would split into a tool, a prompt and its parameters.

  1. 大小Size

    How large you write is how much of the painting changes.

  2. 位置Location

    Where you write is where the change happens.

  3. 语境Context

    What is already there decides whether a character replaces, modifies or adds.

  4. 语义Semantics

    Some characters are pictures. Others change the weather, or the hour.

卷

A session

First a mountain. Then the sun.

器

The instrument

A brush, a cup of water, an easel of light.

Canvas
Semi-translucent matte acrylic, back-projected, so the trace glows from within.
Sensing
An infrared multi-touch frame around the canvas detects the brush.
Brush
A Chinese calligraphy brush and a cup of water: the feel of brushing, without pigment.
Light
A projector behind the canvas, on an adjustable aluminium easel.
Sound
A speaker beside the easel.
Mind
A computer coordinating sensing, recognition, painting and sound.

时

Ten seconds

The waiting is the drying.

Painting a new image takes about ten seconds, long enough to collapse into waiting. So the stroke shows at once and fades like water drying while the painting is made. The delay becomes part of the work: a rhythm of mark, fade and response, closer to following a material than executing a plan.

思

Reflections

What people did with it.

  1. 一

    Easier than a prompt.

    People wrote straight into the area they wanted, instead of describing a place in words. Many characters are near-square, so turning, scaling or combining them reshapes the target.

  2. 二

    A shared game.

    Two groups of high school students agreed together on what to paint next, and cheered when an update landed. When it missed, the gap between intention, recognition and painting became a joke and a puzzle to solve. Some tested the edges with anachronisms and negations: airplane, train, nothing, cancellation.

  3. 三

    Where it goes next.

    Shrink the rectangle to the written shape, so irregular areas can be targeted. Let changes bleed in like ink, not cut. Write short prose or poetry for more abstract answers. Carry the idea to other writing systems.

梦笔生花

The name

Flowers from the tip of a brush.

Dream Brush echoes an old idiom, 梦笔生花. As a boy, the poet Li Bai dreamt that flowers bloomed from the tip of his brush.
