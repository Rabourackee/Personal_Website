01mission briefing

ABOUT.TXT42 lines · 1.4 kb

programmers talk to rubber ducks to reason about code. this one listens, and then fixes your code itself, pecking at the keyboard with its oversized bill.

> hypothesis

We built it to stress-test LLMs in the physical world. The bill can't hit single keys precisely, so the duck constantly makes and corrects errors while replanning its movements. We gave it a grumpy personality: it quacks, complains, and begrudgingly types through its Sisyphean task.

> question

It pulls “vibe coding” into the physical world, and asks: if humans can continuously adapt, re-plan and correct their actions in real time, can AI do the same?

03kinematics

two circles, one neck.

The neck is a four-bar linkage with two degrees of freedom, extension and pecking, driven by two servos. To put the bill on a key, the microcontroller solves inverse kinematics with a compact circle-intersection method. Drag the yellow target.

IK_SOLVER.SIMdrag the target
  1. the shoulder circle.The upper arm pivots at the shoulder, so the elbow must sit somewhere on a circle of radius L₁ around it.
  2. the target circle.The forearm has to end at the target, so the elbow must also sit on a circle of radius L₂ around the target.
  3. where they cross.The two circles meet at two points. Each is a valid elbow, and each is a different pose.
  4. never flip.Always keep the upper one. The neck never jumps between solutions, so motion stays stable, and the maths is light enough to run on the microcontroller.

04hardware

three servos and a bill.

One servo in the base swings the whole neck across the keyboard. Two more drive the four-bar neck: one reaches, one pecks. Everything else is 3D-printed, in several colours.

  1. head frameCarries the duck's head and its oversized bill, the part that actually strikes the keys.
  2. 4-bar linkageTurns two servo rotations into the neck's two motions: extension and pecking.
  3. left + right servosThe neck's two drivers, mounted side by side at the foot of the linkage.
  4. custom connectorsPrinted brackets that lock the neck assembly onto the turntable.
  5. stop pin + gearThe gear carries the base servo's rotation up to the neck; the stop pin keeps the swing within range.
  6. ball bearingsA ring of bearings lets the turntable spin smoothly under the weight of the neck.
  7. base + base servoSwings the whole neck left and right across the keyboard.

05system

a duck with a master mind.

Mechanics, audio, sensing and AI run as separate modules that talk over HTTP. A central server hands out tasks and the modules report back. Claude is the master mind, and the loop only closes when the code on screen is right.

01 / 08
  1. You talk through the bug. The editor turns your voice into a prompt.
  2. The spoken prompt and the current text diff go to the main server: the bridge.
  3. Claude, the master mind, reads the prompt, the diff and any calibration advice.
  4. It answers with a tool path, the keys it means to hit, and a few quips and quacks.
  5. ElevenLabs turns the quips into the duck's grumpy voice.
  6. The firmware runs the tool path. The base swings, the neck reaches, the bill pecks.
  7. An ESP32 camera watches the bill. Gemini compares where it landed with the keys it meant to hit, and suggests a calibration.
  8. The keystrokes land in the editor. The new diff goes back to Claude, and the loop runs again until the code is right.

  4. measuring reach.The neck against a ruler.

  1. make the error the point.A bill can't hit single keys precisely. Instead of hiding that, the duck lives in constant correction. That's the experiment.
  2. give it a personality.Grumpy quips turn every miss into something you watch, not something you wait for.
  3. close the loop with eyes.The editor's text diff says what went wrong; the camera says where the bill actually landed.
  4. keep the brain off the duck.A central server dispatches tasks to separate modules over HTTP, so each part can fail, retry and report on its own.
  5. solve it on the chip.Simulated servo ranges plus a compact IK keep every peck stable and computable on a microcontroller.
